Notice Routing

Where a notice goes after the Compliance Admin publishes it — which roles are mailed, how many mails are actually sent, and why that differs from a compliance.

Who a notice reaches

Recipients are never chosen by hand. They are read from the same tables the compliance approval flow routes through.

The grid's comp_admin column holds the CHD

Despite its name, that column names employee 100803 on all 18 rows, whose login role is role_id 7 (CHD). It is read as a recipient. Actual Compliance Admins are excluded by employee code before any of this runs.

Why 22 people get 13 mails

One mail per audience, not one per person and not one per plant. People who cover the same plants read the same mail, so they share a send.

Ten triggers in total. The TO is whoever must act next; the CC follows one rule, drawn below.

No email address means no mail, and the record is still saved

If a recipient has no valid address the send is skipped and the compliance or notice is committed anyway. That is deliberate — a mail server being unreachable must never destroy work that was already done.

The Authority receives no compliance mail whatsoever

The word AUTHORITY does not appear once in the compliance service. They are never a TO, and the CC list is built from the admin role alone — so nothing about a compliance ever reaches them by mail. Their dashboard is the only way they learn anything, and they must go and look.

Notices are the exact opposite: an Authority is the one role that receives every one, regardless of plant.

Nothing chases a request sitting with an approver

The daily 08:45 job scans findByStatus(0) — submission-pending only. A request that has been submitted and is waiting at Approval Pending (11) or Final Approval Pending (22) is never scanned, so it sends no reminder and never flips to Overdue, however long it sits there.

In practice: due-soon and overdue mails only ever reach the doer. A Comp Head, Group HR Head or HCM Head is never chased.

Publishing one

Comp Admin only, from the same screen that assigns compliances.

  1. Request Type → Notice. The form opens on Compliance; switching swaps Frequency and Due Date out for a Notice Description.
  2. Select Plant, or ALL PLANTS to publish everywhere at once.
  3. Category and Subcategory — the same act masters the compliance form uses. The subcategory becomes the notice's Subject.
  4. Attachment — PDF, Excel, JPG or PNG, up to 50 MB. The file name may use letters, numbers, spaces and . - _ ( ) only.
  5. Notice Description — optional, shown in the mail and on the board.
  6. Submit. Published immediately, mails sent, bell rows written. There is no confirmation step because there is nothing to approve.
ALL PLANTS behaves the opposite way to a compliance

A notice is one notice stored per plant — one number, one document, and one mail per audience. An ALL PLANTS compliance creates 18 separate records, each with its own Sr. No. and approval chain, so a Plant HR covering four plants gets four mails.

No email address means no bell either

A recipient without a valid address is skipped before the bell row is written, so they get neither the mail nor the notification — though the notice is still on their dashboard. A compliance writes the bell row regardless. Worth aligning if it matters.
