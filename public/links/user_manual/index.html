02 The two approval flows

A compliance always travels the same three stages — assign → submit → approve — but who submits and who gives the first approval depends on the compliance subcategory. That is set once per subcategory in Admin Settings (Approval Flow = Plant HR or Compliance Admin) and the Assign screen then picks it up on its own.

A rejection is not the end of the request. Rejecting sends the compliance back to the person who submitted it. It reappears on their Pending tab with the approver's comment in the Action History, ready to be corrected and re-submitted.

03 Opening the portal

There is no separate password to remember. The Compliance Portal trusts the RUCHA portal you are already signed in to.

  1. Sign in to the RUCHA portal as you normally do.
  2. Open Compliance Management from the portal's application list. The portal hands your identity across and the Compliance Portal signs you in automatically — no second login screen.
  3. You land on your role's dashboard. Comp Admin opens on Overdue Compliance (which leads with the Compliance Calendar); every other role opens on its Pending / Approval Pending tab.
"Access Denied" instead of a dashboard? Your employee code has no compliance role mapped to it yet. Ask the Comp Admin to add you in Admin Settings → Login Access; nothing on your side will fix it.
Sessions time out. After a spell of inactivity the portal signs you out and returns you to the RUCHA portal. Re-open Compliance Management to carry on — nothing you had already submitted is lost.

04 What is on your dashboard

Every role's dashboard is built from the same parts. Learn them once here and the rest of this manual is just "which button, on which tab".

Reading the table

  • Compliance Sr. No — the record's identity, e.g. 1003/08/26/936 (plant code / month / year / serial). Click it to open the compliance.
  • Due Date — the date the compliance must be completed by. For an AS & WHEN compliance this is the end date of its window.
  • Status — where the record is right now. When something is waiting on you, the badge says so from your point of view (Submission Pending, Pending, Final Approval Pending); otherwise it shows the record's own state. The full list is in section 16.
Counts and lists always agree. The number on a card is exactly what you will see when you open that tab, including the Time Period filter — so if a card shows 12 and the list shows 12 rows, nothing is hidden.

05 Comp Admin — assigning a compliance

Assigning is how a compliance enters the system. You choose the plant, what the compliance is, how often it recurs and when it is due, and attach the act or notification document. The portal works out who it goes to and tells them.

Filling the form

  1. Select Plant * — start typing to search the list. Choose ALL PLANTS to raise the same compliance for every plant in one go (see the note below).
  2. Compliance Category — the act, e.g. LABOR LAWS. Choosing one loads the subcategories that belong to it.
  3. Compliance Subcategory — the specific requirement. This is also what decides the approval flow, and so who has to do the work: the Compliance Admin route (Flow 1 — CHD submits, Comp Head approves) or the Plant HR route (Flow 2 — the plant's Plant HR submits, Group HR Head approves).
  4. Frequency — Yearly, Half-Yearly, Quarterly or Monthly.
  5. Due Date — click the box, or the calendar icon at its right edge, to open the picker. Past dates cannot be chosen. For an AS & WHEN compliance you get a Start Date and an End Date instead of a single due date; all three boxes carry the same icon.
  6. Attachment * — the compliance act or notification document. This is what the doer reads to know what is expected of them, so it is mandatory.
  7. Submit. A confirmation appears and you are taken to the Pending Compliance tab with the new request at the top of the list.
ALL PLANTS raises one request per plant, one after another. The button shows "Assigning 3 of 16…" while it works — let it finish. If a plant has no Plant HR mapped, that plant is skipped and named in the summary; the rest are still created.
What the doer sees. The moment you submit, the compliance appears on the doer's Pending tab with the badge Submission Pending, their notification bell count goes up, and they are e-mailed.

06 Comp Admin — tracking what you assigned

The three remaining cards are the life of a request: waiting, finished, and late.

TabWhat it holds
Pending Compliance Assigned but not yet closed — awaiting submission, awaiting an approval, or rejected and waiting to be corrected. The Pending At column tells you who is holding it up.
Approved Compliance Finished — approved all the way through HCM Head. Nothing further is required.
Overdue Compliance Past its due date and still not closed. This tab opens with the Compliance Calendar (see section 14).

Deleting a request

Only the Comp Admin's Pending tab carries a delete button, and it is meant for a request raised in error — a wrong plant, a wrong subcategory. You are asked to confirm before anything is removed.

Delete cannot be undone. The request and its history go with it. If work has already been submitted against a compliance, reject it back to the doer with a comment instead of deleting it.

07 CHD / Plant HR — submitting a compliance

This is the doer's job: do what the compliance requires, then record it here with the proof. The CHD does this on Flow 1 and the Plant HR of the assigned plant code does it on Flow 2 — the screen and the steps are the same for both, only the heading differs.

  1. Open the compliance — click the blue serial number. The list is replaced by the compliance detail; BACK TO LIST in the header returns you.
  2. Read section 1, Compliance Act Details — what is required, for which plant, by when. Click the Attachment box to download the act or notification document.
  3. Check section 2, Action History — every step taken so far, newest at the bottom. If this compliance was rejected earlier, the approver's reason is the comment on that row.
  4. Fill section 3, Action — the date the compliance was actually completed, the proof document, and a comment describing what was done. All three are required.
  5. Submit. The compliance leaves your Pending tab and moves to the first approver — Comp Head on Flow 1, Group HR Head (Corp HR) on Flow 2.
No 3.ACTION card? Then nothing is required of you on this record — it is with someone else, or already closed. Opening a compliance from the Approved tab is always read-only.
Overdue tab. A compliance past its due date moves to Overdue, but it does not go away: you submit it from there in exactly the same way.

08 Comp Head — first approval (Flow 1)

Everything the CHD submits stops with you before it can reach the HCM Head.

  1. Open the record from the Approval Pending tab.
  2. Check the evidence — section 2, Action History, holds the submitter's comment and their attachment. Click the file name to download it.
  3. Choose APPROVE or REJECT in section 3 and enter a comment — the comment is required either way, and it is what the next person (or the submitter, if you reject) reads.
  4. Submit. Approve sends it to HCM Head for final approval; Reject sends it back to the submitter to be corrected and re-submitted.
Approving does not close the compliance. It moves to Final Approval Pending and appears on the HCM Head's list. It is only finished when they approve it too.

09 Group HR Head (Corp HR) — first approval (Flow 2)

Your role is the Comp Head's opposite number on Flow 2: you approve what the plants submit, for every plant mapped to you. The portal labels your dashboard CORP HR COMPLIANCE DASHBOARD.

The steps are identical to section 8: open the record, read the plant's comment and download their attachment from the Action History, then choose APPROVE or REJECT with a comment. Approving passes it to HCM Head; rejecting returns it to the plant.

Reject with a usable reason. Your comment is the only instruction the plant gets. "Wrong document — challan required, not the receipt" saves a round trip; "not ok" does not.

10 HCM Head — final approval

Both flows end at your desk. Approving here closes the compliance for good.

  1. Open the record from Approval Pending.
  2. Read the whole chain in Action History — who submitted, what they attached, and what the first approver said. Every step is on that one table.
  3. Approve or reject with a comment. Approve marks the compliance Approved and moves it to everyone's Approved tab. Reject sends it all the way back to the original submitter.

11 Authority — viewing compliances

The Authority dashboard is for oversight. You can see and export everything; you are never asked to act, and no action form is offered when you open a record.

  • Pending — everything still in flight, with who is holding it.
  • Approved — closed compliances, and those already cleared by the first approver and awaiting final approval.
  • Rejected — sent back for correction, with the reason in the record's Action History.
  • Overdue — past due and still open. This is the tab to watch.

12 Notices

A notice is an announcement, not a task. It is published once, it reaches the people responsible for the plants it names, and it is read. Nobody submits it, nobody approves it, and it never appears on anyone's Pending list.

A notice is not a compliance Everything else in this manual describes work that moves through an approval flow. A notice has no flow at all — no due date, no frequency, no approver, no status. If something needs an action from somebody, it is a compliance, not a notice.

Publishing a notice — Comp Admin

Notices are raised from the same screen as compliances. Open Assign Compliance / Notice and set the first field, Request Type, to Notice. The form below it changes to suit.

  1. Request Type * — choose Notice. It opens on Compliance, which is the everyday case.
  2. Select Plant * — one plant, or ALL PLANTS to publish to every plant at once.
  3. Compliance Category and Compliance Subcategory — the same two lists the compliance form uses. They say what the notice is about, and the subcategory becomes the notice's Subject.
  4. Attachment — optional on a notice, unlike a compliance, where it is required. Publish an announcement with nothing attached, or attach the document: PDF, Excel, JPG or PNG, up to 50 MB. File names may use letters, numbers, spaces and . - _ ( ) only.
  5. Notice Description — optional. A short line of context, shown in the mail and on the board.
  6. Submit — the notice is published immediately and the mails go out. There is no confirmation step, because there is nothing to approve.

There is no Frequency and no Due Date on a notice: it happens once, so it has no deadline and never appears on the Compliance Calendar. The Attachment is optional here and required there. Everything else on the form behaves exactly as it does for a compliance (see section 5).

Who receives a notice

The recipients are read from the plant's row in the reporting grid — the same mapping the approval flow uses to decide who approves a compliance. Nobody is chosen by hand.

RoleReceives a notice for…

One mail per person, not one per plant If you cover seven plants and a notice goes to ALL PLANTS, you get one mail listing your seven plants — not seven mails, and not a mail naming all eighteen. This is the opposite of a compliance: an ALL PLANTS compliance creates a separate record per plant, each with its own Sr. No. and its own approval chain, so it sends you one mail per plant.

Reading notices — everybody

  • Open it from the NOTICE card on any dashboard. The number on the card is how many notices you can currently see.
  • The table lists the notice number, subject, description, plant, category, who published it and when. ALL PLANTS in the plant column means the notice went to every plant.
  • opens the attached document in a new tab; saves it. The document is not attached to the mail — it lives here.
  • The Filter By Period bar narrows the list by the date a notice was published. It is the same filter the compliance dashboards use, and a year or month you pick here still applies when you click back to a compliance tab.
  • EXCEL / PDF / PRINT export exactly what the table currently shows.

The bell

A new notice raises a bell notification for everyone it was sent to, shown with a icon. Clicking it opens the Notice Dashboard. Because the board shows every notice at once, opening it marks them all read and the count clears — unlike a compliance notification, which clears only when you open that particular record.

Withdrawing a notice — Comp Admin

Published something by mistake? Open the Notice Dashboard from the Comp Admin dashboard and each row carries a button. It asks to confirm, then removes the notice for everybody — all its plant rows, its document, and the bell notifications that announced it.

Withdrawing is permanent, and the mail has already gone There is no undo and no draft state. Removing a notice takes it off every dashboard, but it cannot recall the mail people have already received — if the notice was wrong, publish a correction as well.

The button appears only when you reach the board from the Comp Admin dashboard. If you hold another role as well and arrive from that dashboard instead, the board is read-only — which is what everybody else always sees.

13 Legal notices

A legal notice runs the opposite way to everything else in this manual. A compliance is handed down — the Comp Admin assigns it and somebody below does the work. A legal notice is handed up: the Plant HR raises it against their own plant, closes it out, and sends it to the Comp Admin for approval. Nobody assigns it to them.

Three different things, easily confused A compliance is work assigned to somebody and approved in two stages. A notice is an announcement with no flow at all. A legal notice is work the Plant HR raises themselves and the Comp Admin signs off — one approver, not two, and no Comp Head or HCM Head anywhere in it.

Raising one — Plant HR

Open Legal Notice in the sidebar, then the ADD LEGAL NOTICE card.

  1. Select Plant * — only the plants you are Plant HR of. If you hold just one it is already filled in and cannot be changed.
  2. Category and Subcategory * — the same two act lists the compliance form uses. Together they become the notice's subject; you are not asked to write one.
  3. Due Date * — when it must be closed by. Dates before today are greyed out and refused.
  4. Attachment * — the notice document itself. PDF, Excel, JPG or PNG, up to 50 MB.
  5. Legal Notice Description — optional, and worth writing: it becomes the first remark in the Action History.
  6. Submit — it is numbered LNTC/<plant>/MM/YY/nnn and appears on your Pending card, waiting for you.
Raising it is not submitting it A newly raised legal notice sits at Submission Pending — with you, not the admin. Nothing reaches the Comp Admin until you open it and take action.

Submitting it — Plant HR

Open the notice from the LEGAL NOTICE NO badge in the list. Card 3, ACTION, asks for three things: the date of submission (today by default), the document proving it was dealt with, and a comment. Submitting moves it to Approval Pending and mails the Comp Admin.

Approving it — Comp Admin

The admin opens the same screen and gets APPROVE or REJECT with a comment. Approving closes the notice and stamps a closing date. Rejecting sends it back to the Plant HR as Submission Pending again — the notice is not finished, and the loop can run as many times as needed. Each submission keeps its own document, so a re-submission never overwrites the first.

No mail on this flow is ever copied to anyone: there is no CC. The view-only roles hear about a legal notice exactly once, when it is raised — they can watch its progress on the dashboard, but they are not told again as it moves.

A bell notification opens the notice itself, and clicking one clears only that notice's entries. Unlike the Notice Dashboard, arriving at the legal notice list does not mark everything read — each notice is opened on its own.

The cards

  • Plant HR — Add Legal Notice, Pending, Approved, Overdue.
  • Comp Admin — Pending, Approved, Overdue, Rejected. There is no Add card: the admin approves legal notices, they do not raise them.
  • Pending is everything unfinished. Overdue is a notice whose due date has passed and which is not yet approved — the day after the due date, not on it.
  • Rejected notices leave the admin's Pending, because the notice is back with the Plant HR. For the Plant HR they stay in Pending, because it is work they still owe.
A legal notice is never deleted There is no withdraw button. Once raised it stays on the record with its full Action History, whether it ends approved or is still going round the rejection loop.

The calendar on this screen

The Legal Notice screen has its own CALENDAR button, beside the title. It is the same month view described in section 14, and which records it draws depends on what you do with notices:

  • Plant HR and Comp Admin — the people who act on notices — get a calendar of legal notices only. Their compliances have their own calendar on the compliance screens, and keeping the two apart means each button shows the work that screen is about.
  • Everyone who only reads notices — Comp Head, Group HR Head, CHD — gets a combined calendar here: compliances and legal notices together, notices in red. They have nothing to do on a notice, so a separate calendar of them would be a month view with no actions in it.

Clicking a legal notice opens it on this screen; clicking a compliance takes you to the compliance screen for your role. BACK TO CALENDAR returns you to the month you came from, and switching tabs closes the calendar rather than leaving it behind the list.

HCM Head and Authority have no calendar button, here or on their compliance screens. Both are oversight roles reading finished or nearly-finished work, and a month view of due dates is there to catch a deadline before it slips — which is a submitter's and an approver's job, not theirs.

14 Everyday tools

These work the same way on every dashboard, whichever role you hold.

The notification bell

  • The red count is the number of unread items that require action — not a total of all records.
  • Opening a compliance marks its notification read, however you got there.
  • Clicking a line opens the record on its list, with the tabs, filters and calendar still around it — so Back returns you to the list rather than to a dead end. You can click a second notification without closing the first.
  • A request assigned with a frequency creates one compliance per period — a monthly one assigned in September creates four, for September to December — and the bell shows one line for each. The mail that accompanies it is a single message.
  • The bell can only report what your dashboards have loaded, so it settles a moment after the page opens.

The Time Period filter

The bar under the cards, marked TIME PERIOD, narrows everything on the dashboard — the table you are looking at and every card count — to a year, a month, or both. Records are matched on their due date, and the filter applies on every tab: Pending, Approved, Rejected and Overdue alike. Choosing a month on its own spans every year (useful for "every March filing"). The button clears it, and the filter resets when you move to a different role's dashboard.

Search, columns and export

  • Search — free text across the whole table.
  • Column filters — narrow one column at a time.
  • Columns — hide columns you do not need; useful before printing.
  • EXCEL / PDF / PRINT — export exactly what the table currently shows, filters included.

The Compliance Calendar

The calendar is a month view of outstanding compliances, laid out on their due dates, so a cluster of deadlines is obvious at a glance. Each entry is a coloured dot and its compliance category; a day with more than a few shows +n MORE.

The calendar is not the tab you opened it from. It draws every compliance still outstanding — pending, submitted, re-submitted, approval pending, rejected and overdue — whichever tab you toggle it on. That is deliberate: a calendar of overdue work only ever tells you what you have already missed, and the point of a month view is to see a deadline before it slips. Approved compliances are the one thing left out; they are finished, and have no deadline left to warn about.

It is reached from the COMPLIANCE CALENDAR button at the top right of the list, and shows the same month wherever you open it. Four roles have it:

RoleWhere the button isWhat the calendar draws

Comp Head Pending, Approved, Rejected Compliances and legal notices

CHD / Plant HR Pending, Approved, Overdue A Plant HR sees compliances only — their legal notices have their own calendar on the Legal Notice screen. A CHD, who only reads notices, sees both here.

Legal notices are drawn in red and prefixed LNTC/, so they stand out from compliances at a glance. Clicking one opens it on the Legal Notice screen for your role, not in the Compliance View — they are different records with different actions.

  1. It opens by itself on Overdue. Signing in as Comp Admin lands you on Overdue Compliance with the calendar already open, rather than the list. The same happens to anyone who also holds Comp Admin alongside another role — Comp Admin decides where they land — and each time you come back to the Comp Admin dashboard from elsewhere. On the other three screens the button is there but the list leads.
  2. Click a compliance in the calendar to open it. The Compliance View replaces the calendar in place, exactly as it does when you open a record from a list.
  3. BACK TO CALENDAR returns you to the month view you came from — the button in the card header knows you arrived from the calendar and says so, instead of BACK TO LIST.
  4. The COMPLIANCE CALENDAR button switches between the calendar and the table whenever you want; moving to another tab shows that tab's list as normal.
The Time Period filter reaches the calendar too. Narrowing to a year or month narrows the calendar along with everything else, so one selection still governs the whole dashboard.

15 Month-end reminder mail

On the last morning of every month the portal writes to everybody who still has something outstanding. You do not have to do anything to receive it, and there is nothing to switch on.

When The last day of every month at 9:00 AM — 30 September, 31 October, 28 or 29 February. It works the month out itself.
Who gets one Anybody holding a compliance role who has pending or overdue work. If you are clear, you get nothing — there is no "you have 0 pending" mail.
What it contains Your counts, and a link to the portal. No attachment.
Copies None. The mail goes to you alone.

What the mail says

The subject names only the flows you actually have, so a Plant HR with no open legal notice does not see them mentioned:

  • IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED ON PENDING / OVERDUE COMPLIANCES
  • … COMPLIANCES & LEGAL NOTICES — when you have both

Inside is a small table of your own figures, and they are the same numbers as the cards on your dashboard. If the mail says 15 pending and 17 overdue, that is what you will find when you log in. Nothing is added up across people.

Approvers have no Overdue figure, and the cell shows a dash rather than a zero. Only a compliance nobody has submitted can turn overdue by its due date passing. Once it has been submitted it is waiting on an approver, and the flow never calls that overdue — which is also why your dashboard has no Overdue card if you are a Comp Head, Group HR Head or HCM Head. A zero there would read as "nothing of yours is late", a claim the system does not make.

The Comp Admin summary

The Comp Admins additionally receive one oversight mail with a spreadsheet attached, Month_End_Pending_<Month><Year>.xlsx. It lists every responsible person with their four figures, exactly as each of them sees it. This is the only mail that carries an attachment, and it is sent every month whether or not anything is outstanding — a report that went quiet when the news was good could not be told apart from one that had stopped running.

A Comp Admin who also holds a compliance role receives two mails: their own reminder, and the summary. They answer different questions.

The total on that sheet is a sum of dashboards, not the size of the backlog. A compliance sitting with a submitter and an approver appears on both their cards, so the column counts it twice. The label on the total row says so. Use the per-person rows for chasing; do not read the total as a record count.
